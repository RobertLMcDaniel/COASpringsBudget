2026 Florida Constitutional Amendment

Property Tax Amendment 3

House Joint Resolution 1F Article VII & Article XII Effective Jan. 1, 2027 if approved

Amendment 3 proposes changes to property assessment limits, homestead exemptions and the permitted uses of certain county and municipal property-tax revenues.

At a glance

Changes to assessments, exemptions and local property-tax revenue.

The resolution also directs the Legislature to create a trust fund to assist with implementation if the amendment is adopted.

If approved Jan. 1, 2027

The constitutional changes described in the resolution would take effect on this date.

What would change

Four major parts of the proposal

These summaries are based on House Joint Resolution 1F and are intended as plain-language explanations, not individualized tax advice.

01 · Assessment limits

Some non-homestead assessment increases would be capped at 5%.

Beginning January 1, 2027, the annual assessment increase limit for specified non-homestead real property would change from 10% to 5% for levies other than school district levies.

02 · Homestead exemption

The homestead exemption would increase in stages.

The proposed language provides an exemption up to $150,000 of assessed value beginning January 1, 2027, and up to $250,000 beginning January 1, 2028.

03 · New Florida residents

A separate five-year rule would apply to certain new residents.

A person establishing Florida residency on or after January 1, 2027, who was not a permanent Florida resident on December 31, 2026, would initially receive an exemption up to $50,000 and generally become eligible for the larger exemption after the five-year period described in the resolution.

04 · Local revenue

County and municipal property-tax revenue would be limited to specified uses.

The proposal identifies permitted uses including public safety, education and public schools, infrastructure, natural-resource projects, certain bonds and debt service, employee retirement obligations and specified county government operations.

Homestead timeline

How the exemption changes over time

The resolution includes separate provisions for existing Florida permanent residents and certain people establishing Florida residency beginning in 2027.

Before 2027
Current structure

The existing constitutional homestead-exemption structure remains in place before the proposed effective date.

January 1, 2027
$150,000

The proposed language increases the exemption for eligible homestead property up to $150,000 of assessed value.

January 1, 2028
$250,000

The proposed language increases the exemption again, up to $250,000 of assessed value.

Important: The resolution contains additional eligibility, inflation-adjustment and implementation provisions. Individual tax effects depend on assessed value, exemptions and the applicable taxing authorities.

Frequently asked questions

Amendment 3 FAQ

The resolution does not calculate an individual property owner's savings or a specific fiscal impact for Altamonte Springs.

What is Amendment 3?
Amendment 3 is a proposed amendment to the Florida Constitution addressing property-tax assessments, homestead exemptions and the use of certain local ad valorem tax revenue. The proposal comes from House Joint Resolution 1F.
When would the amendment take effect?
If approved by voters, the constitutional changes described in the resolution would take effect January 1, 2027.
Does the proposal immediately eliminate all property taxes?
No. The resolution does not make all property taxes disappear on January 1, 2027. It increases the homestead exemption in stages and directs the Legislature to establish procedures relating to additional exemption increases. The official ballot summary describes a schedule for full elimination through general law.
How would the homestead exemption change?
For eligible homestead property, the proposed language provides an exemption up to $150,000 beginning January 1, 2027, and up to $250,000 beginning January 1, 2028. The resolution includes additional eligibility and inflation-adjustment provisions.
What happens if someone becomes a Florida resident after January 1, 2027?
A person who establishes Florida residency on or after January 1, 2027, and was not a permanent Florida resident as of December 31, 2026, would generally receive an exemption up to $50,000 during the initial period. The larger exemption would become available after the five-year requirement described in the resolution, subject to the amendment's other provisions.
Does Amendment 3 change the annual assessment cap on non-homestead property?
Yes. For specified non-homestead real property, the proposal changes the annual assessment-increase cap from 10% to 5% beginning January 1, 2027. That proposed 5% limitation applies to levies other than school district levies.
What could counties and municipalities use property-tax revenue for?
The proposal lists specified uses including public safety, education and public schools, infrastructure, natural-resource projects, eligible bonds and debt service, retirement obligations for local government employees and certain county government operations.
What is the proposed state trust fund?
If the amendment is adopted, the resolution directs the Legislature to create a trust fund to provide grants assisting with implementation. The ballot summary describes the fund as supporting core local services such as education, law enforcement and infrastructure.
Does the resolution state how much Altamonte Springs would gain or lose?
No. House Joint Resolution 1F does not provide a dollar-specific fiscal impact for the City of Altamonte Springs. Any City-specific financial analysis would be separate from the text of the amendment.
Is the City of Altamonte Springs supporting or opposing Amendment 3?
This webpage is designed as an informational resource only. It does not advocate for or against Amendment 3.
